and results for Experiments 1, 2, and 3 of the \texttt{kernel-active-bandit}
benchmark suite, \textbf{as they currently stand after a substantial

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Experiment 1 --- Gaussian mixtures: competitiveness in the parametric regime}

\paragraph{Claim tested.} In the mean-separated Gaussian setting (home turf of
mean-based methods like ACB), KABC's budget is within a modest factor of ACB's;
budget scales as $s_*^{-2}\log(N/\delta)$ (the paper's Theorem 3.1). Also
(current redesign): how conservative each adaptive method's own stopping
rule is, and whether active/adaptive sampling beats naive uniform sampling
at a matched budget.

\paragraph{Instance family.} \texttt{GaussianMixtureEnv}: $N$ arms in $K=3$ balanced
clusters, arm $i$ in cluster $k$ distributed as $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma^2 I_d)$,
$\sigma=1$. Cluster means $\mu_k = \frac{\Delta}{\sqrt2}(e_{2k-1}+e_{2k})$, giving each
pair of cluster means an actual Euclidean separation of exactly $\Delta$.

\paragraph{Grid.} One knob swept at a time, others held at the base config
$N{=}60, K{=}3, d{=}10, \Delta{=}3$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta \in \{1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ (6 points --- the headline sweep)
  \item $d \in \{6, 10, 50, 100\}$ (4 points; $d=2$ excluded, construction requires $d \ge 2K=6$)
  \item $N \in \{30, 60, 120\}$ (3 points canonical; this report's pilot also adds $N=90$ as a 14th point)
\end{itemize}
13 canonical grid points (the base config is each sweep's own midpoint, so it
is run identically under all three sweep tags --- a documented, accepted
redundancy), 14 in this pilot run.

\paragraph{Algorithms: 8 rows per (grid point, rep).} Three independent BASE
algorithms --- \texttt{kabc}, \texttt{kabc-sg}, \texttt{acb} --- each run
once at the grid point's full \texttt{budget\_cap}. Each base is then,
separately, re-run at $\text{budget\_cap} = \min(0.9 \times \tau_{\text{own}},
\text{budget\_cap})$ with a fresh independent RNG substream ---
\texttt{kabc-0.9x}, \texttt{kabc-sg-0.9x}, \texttt{acb-0.9x} respectively,
each testing how conservative THAT algorithm's own stopping rule is.
\texttt{unif-kkm-1x} and \texttt{unif-kkm-0.9x} are tied specifically to
\texttt{kabc}'s own $\tau$ (not kabc-sg's or acb's), testing whether
active/adaptive selection beats naive uniform sampling at a matched budget.
No \texttt{tas-fw} here: its finite-alphabet assumption does not hold for
continuous Gaussian instances (Experiment~3 only).

\paragraph{Budget\_cap.} One value per grid point, shared across all 8
algorithm rows and every rep: $\text{budget\_cap} = 200\, N\, s_*^{-2}
\log(N/\delta)$ (Theorem~3.1), $s_*^2$ Monte-Carlo-estimated
(\texttt{kabc.metrics.estimate\_s\_star\_sq}).

\subsection{Experiment 2 --- Identical means, different shapes: nonparametric separation}
\label{sec:setup-exp2}

\paragraph{Claim tested.} When clusters share the same mean, mean-based
methods are blind (ARI at chance for any budget) while KABC recovers the
partition with budget governed by the MMD gap. Also (current redesign): the
same stopping-rule-conservativeness and active-vs-uniform-sampling questions
as Experiment 1.

\paragraph{Instance families and the current 6-setting grid.} Three
families, $N=40$, $K=2$ throughout. As of this report, the grid is
\textbf{6 fixed, illustrative settings} (not a sweep whose in-between
variation is the point --- see \texttt{docs/experiments.md}'s Experiment 2
GRID TRIM NOTE for the rationale):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{scale\_pair}} (2a): cluster A $=\mathcal N(0,1)$, cluster B
    $=\mathcal N(0,\sigma_B^2)$ --- same mean, different \emph{variance}.
    $\sigma_B \in \{2, 3\}$.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{moment\_matched\_mixture}} (2b): cluster A $=\mathcal
    N(0,1)$, cluster B $= 0.5\,\mathcal N(-a,b^2) + 0.5\,\mathcal N(a,b^2)$,
    $b^2=1-a^2$ --- same mean \emph{and} variance, different \emph{shape}.
    $a \in \{0.85, 0.9\}$.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{ring\_blob}} (2c): cluster A $=$ a 2D ring (Gaussian
    noise $\sigma=0.1$), cluster B $=\mathcal N(0,I_2)$, both centered at the
    origin --- same mean, different \emph{2D geometry}. Ring radius $r \in
    \{2, 3\}$.
\end{itemize}
50 repetitions per setting (raised from an earlier 20, to match this
report's production run).

\paragraph{Algorithms: 8 rows per (setting, rep), identical scheme to
Experiment 1.} Same base/0.9x-self-comparison/unif-kkm-tied-to-kabc
structure as Experiment 1 (see above). No \texttt{kabc-oracle} (dropped ---
its one-shot required budget exceeded \texttt{budget\_cap} at every grid
point of the old design, so the ``price of adaptivity'' figure it existed
for never produced real data). No \texttt{tas-fw}, same reason as
Experiment 1.

\paragraph{Budget\_cap.} Identical mechanism to Experiment 1: one
$s_*$-formula value per setting, shared across all 8 rows and every rep.

\subsection{Experiment 3 --- MovieLens random pairs: robustness across the real-data pair distribution}

\paragraph{Claim tested.} Whether KABC's advantage (and ACB's/TaS-FW's
relative behavior) holds up when the movie PAIR is randomly resampled every
repetition across the real diversity of the MovieLens catalog, under three
distinct pairing regimes that vary how much mean separation is present and
whether the second movie is cherry-picked (max MMD$^2$) or sampled
uniformly. Also: how conservative KABC's own stopping rule is.

\paragraph{Data.} \texttt{data/ml-1m-207/} --- the 207 MovieLens-1M movies
with $\ge 1000$ ratings. Discrete rating support $\{1,\ldots,5\}$,
RBF-on-ratings kernel, fixed median-heuristic bandwidth ($=1.0$) computed
once over the full 207-movie pool.

\paragraph{Instance.} $N=8$ arms, $K=2$ clusters, 4 arms per prototype
(movie), \texttt{MovieLensPairEnv}. For a given rep, a single shared
permutation of all 207 movie indices is drawn; each of the three versions
independently scans that same permutation and picks the first candidate
movie A with at least one eligible B under \emph{its own} gap rule (so
versions usually, but not always, land on the same A --- see
\texttt{docs/experiments.md} for the precise mechanism).

\paragraph{Version-specific B-selection rules.}
\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{exp3\_1} --- tight mean gap, MMD-maximizing.] Candidates: all
    movies with $|\text{mean}_A - \text{mean}_B| \le 0.25$. $B = \arg\max
    \mathrm{MMD}^2$ among candidates.
  \item[\texttt{exp3\_2} --- moderate-to-large mean gap, uniform.]
    Candidates: all movies with $0.25 \le |\text{mean}_A - \text{mean}_B|
    \le 1.0$. $B$ sampled uniformly at random among candidates.
  \item[\texttt{exp3\_3} --- tight mean gap, uniform.] Candidates: all movies
    with $|\text{mean}_A - \text{mean}_B| \le 0.25$ (same tolerance as
    \texttt{exp3\_1}, a clean matched-tolerance ablation of cherry-picking
    vs.\ uniform B-selection). $B$ sampled uniformly at random.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Budget.} Fixed $\text{budget\_cap}=15{,}000$ pulls (not the
$s_*$-formula type --- not meaningful at this instance scale). Deliberately
not raised to clear \texttt{kabc}'s doubling-trick round-7 threshold
($28{,}560$ at $N=8,\delta=0.05$): this leaves \texttt{kabc}'s own
non-convergent behavior unchanged while giving \texttt{acb}/\texttt{tas-fw}
less runway.

\paragraph{Algorithms: 6 rows per (rep, version).} \texttt{kabc},
\texttt{kabc-0.9x}, \texttt{acb}, \texttt{tas-fw}, \texttt{unif-kkm-1x},
\texttt{unif-kkm-0.9x} --- the same base/0.9x/unif-kkm-tied-to-kabc scheme
as Experiments 1--2, restricted to the three algorithms for which it is
meaningful here (\texttt{kabc-sg} dropped this round --- its fragility on
this exact real-data setting is already thoroughly documented from a prior
run, see \texttt{docs/experiments.md}'s REVISION 4 note).

\paragraph{Status.} \textbf{Pending a fresh production run at this
report's writing time} (Run Provenance paragraph above) --- Section~\ref{sec:results-exp3}
below is a placeholder.

\section{Baseline Algorithms}
\label{sec:baselines}

\subsection{KABC and KABC-SG (this paper's own methods)}

KABC (Algorithm~2 of \cite{kabc2026}) runs a doubling-trick outer loop: at
iteration $k$, pull $n_k = \lceil 2^k \log(8(N^2-N)/\delta_k)\rceil$ fresh samples
per arm ($\delta_k = \delta/(4k^2)$), then build a same-cluster graph over all arm
pairs via a pairwise MMD-based test --- an edge $\{i,j\}$ is added iff the
empirical MMD statistic between arms $i$ and $j$ falls under a threshold. The
recovered partition is the graph's connected components; the loop stops the
first time the number of connected components equals the true $K$.

Two threshold variants are compared (identical outer loop, different pairwise
test):
\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{kabc}] variance-aware, Wolfer--Alquier-style threshold (Eq.~6 of
    \cite{kabc2026}): sizes each pair's own threshold from that pair's
    \emph{empirical} RKHS variance, so a noisier pair automatically gets more
    slack.
  \item[\texttt{kabc-sg}] kernel-uniform sub-Gaussian threshold (the Discussion
    section's ablation in \cite{kabc2026}): uses only the fixed kernel bound
    (RBF kernel sup $=1$), identical for every pair regardless of that pair's
    own spread --- the ablation whose practical fragility Section~\ref{sec:conclusions}
    discusses in detail.
\end{description}

\paragraph{The \texttt{-0.9x} self-comparison rows (current redesign, all
three experiments).} For each base algorithm (\texttt{kabc},
\texttt{kabc-sg}, \texttt{acb}), a second, independent run at
$\text{budget\_cap} = \min(0.9\times\tau_{\text{own}}, \text{budget\_cap})$
tests how much margin that algorithm's own stopping rule carries: a small
ARI drop from the base row to its own \texttt{-0.9x} row means little margin
was needed (the bound is comfortably tight relative to what was actually
necessary); a large drop means real accuracy was still being bought in that
last 10\% of budget. This replaces an earlier, cruder
$\text{budget\_multiplier}\in\{1.0,0.25\}$ restricted-budget pass applied
uniformly across all algorithms.

\subsection{ACB --- Active Clustering with Bandit feedback}

Source: \cite{acb2024}. ACB* (this repo's adaptive variant, ``$\theta_*$ and
$\Delta_*$ unknown,'' referred to throughout as ACB) is a \emph{mean}-based active
clustering procedure (SRI + ADC subroutines): it estimates each arm's mean
directly (no kernel/MMD machinery) and clusters by Euclidean distance between
empirical means, adaptively refining which arms to pull based on estimated
separation. It is the ``home turf'' competitor for Experiment~1 (mean-separated
Gaussians, exactly ACB's design setting) and the mean-blindness foil for
Experiments~2--3 (same-mean, different-shape data, where a mean-only method has no
signal by construction).

\subsection{TaS-FW --- Track-and-Stop with Frank-Wolfe}

Source: \cite{tasfw2024}. Track-and-Stop based on Frank-Wolfe optimization, for
$N$-ary clustering of arms whose distributions live on a common \emph{finite}
alphabet. Only run in Experiment~3, where MovieLens ratings on
$\{1,\ldots,5\}$ satisfy the finite-alphabet assumption natively; not run on
Experiments~1--2's continuous instances, which would require an off-label
discretization approximation outside the algorithm's proven guarantees. Its
Track-and-Stop design re-scans its entire hypothesis space and re-solves a
linear program at \emph{every single pull} (not periodically, like KABC's
doubling trick), which is why Experiment~3 caps $N$ at 8: the hypothesis
space grows combinatorially in $N$, and empirically \texttt{tas-fw} already
costs two to three orders of magnitude more wall-clock per repetition than
every other algorithm at $N=8$.

\subsection{Unif-KKM --- uniform sampling + kernel $k$-means (folklore baseline)}

No paper --- a standard, non-adaptive baseline implemented directly per the
design doc. Pulls a fixed number of samples per arm, then runs ordinary
kernel $k$-means on the resulting empirical kernel mean embeddings. Has no
confidence-based stopping rule and no $\delta$-PAC guarantee --- it always
spends its entire allotted budget and always ``completes''
($\text{stopped}=\text{True}$ by construction), so it is included purely as
a non-adaptive reference point, not as a $\delta$-correct competitor.
\textbf{Current redesign:} unlike the earlier design (an independent $n$-sweep
of its own), \texttt{unif-kkm} no longer sweeps an independent budget --- its
two rows, \texttt{unif-kkm-1x} and \texttt{unif-kkm-0.9x}, are tied
specifically to \texttt{kabc}'s own realized $\tau$ for that same rep
($\text{budget\_cap} = \min(1.0\text{ or }0.9 \times \tau_{\texttt{kabc}},
\text{budget\_cap})$), directly testing whether active/adaptive selection
beats naive uniform sampling at a matched budget, rather than reporting
Unif-KKM's own independent budget-accuracy curve.

\section{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

\texttt{kabc}, \texttt{kabc-sg}, and \texttt{acb} each have a theoretically-derived
but conservative leading constant in their stopping threshold. Per the
calibration protocol (\texttt{docs/experiments.md} \S0.2), each constant was
empirically calibrated once (binary search for the smallest constant keeping
empirical error $\le \delta/2$, validated under the REAL formula-derived
budget regime, not an artificially generous one), then frozen and reused
unchanged across every experiment. The frozen values actually used for this
report (unchanged from the previous report):

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Algorithm & $c_{\text{cal}}$ & Calibration-set error & Held-out error \\
\midrule
\texttt{kabc} & 0.103516 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
\texttt{kabc-sg} & 0.121094 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
\texttt{acb} & 0.658984 & 0.020 & 0.010 \\
\texttt{tas-fw} & --- (no calibratable constant) & --- & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Important caveat, carried forward from the previous report.}
Calibration instances are a \emph{single} fixed Gaussian-mixture instance
(\texttt{GaussianMixtureEnv}'s construction is deterministic given
$N,K,d,\Delta$ --- there is no way to draw ``20 different instances,'' only
20 independently-seeded noise draws of the one instance). The frozen
constants above were validated against that one instance's own real budget
regime, \emph{not} against every real grid point in Experiments~1--3.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} shows this still matters directly:
\texttt{kabc-sg}'s calibration does not fully generalize to the harder grid
points/settings in either experiment below.

\paragraph{Where \texttt{kabc-sg} struggles.} At $\Delta=1$ (the hardest
separation), \texttt{kabc-sg} never converges within budget in this pilot
(0/10 reps stopped) and its mean $\tau$ balloons to $277{,}440$ --- the
doubling trick's ceiling at this $N,\delta$ --- while \texttt{kabc} converges
every time at $\tau=30{,}840$. The same pattern recurs at the two hardest
$d$ points ($d=50,100$): \texttt{kabc-sg} never converges (0/10 both), mean
$\tau=277{,}440$ both times, while \texttt{kabc} converges every time at a
small fraction of that budget ($14{,}100$). \texttt{acb} converges
everywhere in this pilot but with real error: $11.4\%$ aggregate, concentrated
at the hardest points.

\paragraph{The \texttt{-0.9x} rows show real margin, not just noise.} Every
base algorithm's ARI drops substantially at 90\% of its own realized
$\tau$: \texttt{kabc} $1.000\to0.027$, \texttt{kabc-sg} $0.881\to0.458$,
\texttt{acb} $0.984\to0.247$. This says that, at this pilot's scale (10
reps), none of the three stopping rules carry much slack --- cutting to 90\%
of the budget each rule itself decided it needed meaningfully hurts accuracy
for all three, not just the already-fragile \texttt{kabc-sg}. \texttt{unif-kkm-0.9x}
barely moves ($1.000\to$ effectively unchanged, since it is tied to
\texttt{kabc}'s $\tau$, not its own, and 90\% of \texttt{kabc}'s ample budget
is still plenty for non-adaptive kernel $k$-means at these easy-to-moderate
grid points).

\paragraph{ACB is exactly at its theoretical budget bound everywhere, never
converging.} Figure~\ref{fig:exp2-fig1} shows \texttt{acb}'s bar coinciding

construction, so a mean-based method has no signal, spends its entire
allotted budget every time, and always falls back to the graceful trivial
partition (ARI exactly 0.0 by construction of the adjusted-for-chance
formula).

\paragraph{KABC-SG is family-dependent, and \texttt{moment\_matched\_mixture}
is uniformly the hardest family.} \texttt{kabc-sg} converges well on
\texttt{scale\_pair} ($\sigma_B{=}3$: 42\% converged but ARI 0.923 when it
does; $\sigma_B{=}2$: 84\%, ARI 0.758) and \texttt{ring\_blob} (86--96\%
converged, ARI 0.989--0.997), but its ARI collapses specifically on
\texttt{moment\_matched\_mixture} (92--98\% converged, but ARI only
0.179--0.234) --- it \emph{converges} confidently to a wrong-ish answer on
this family far more than it fails to converge, the same
confident-wrong-answer failure mode documented for this algorithm on real
MovieLens data in the earlier (now-superseded) Experiment 3/4 report.
\texttt{kabc}, by contrast, is at ARI $1.000$ on every one of the 6 settings
individually, not just on average.

\paragraph{The \texttt{-0.9x} rows again show real margin, most sharply for
\texttt{kabc} itself.} \texttt{kabc}'s own ARI at 90\% of its realized
$\tau$ drops to just $0.123$ aggregate --- markedly worse than Experiment 1's
already-weak $0.027$-at-90\%-margin story is not directly comparable in
scale, but the direction is the same: KABC's stopping rule is not leaving
much slack on this benchmark. \texttt{unif-kkm-1x}/\texttt{-0.9x}, both
tied to \texttt{kabc}'s own (ample, MMD-driven) $\tau$, reach ARI
$0.987$/$0.988$ --- close to but not quite matching \texttt{kabc}'s perfect
$1.000$, suggesting active/adaptive selection's edge over naive uniform
sampling at a matched budget is real here, but modest in this aggregate
view (see the per-setting figures for where it is largest).

\section{Inferences and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\paragraph{6.1 KABC's empirical error remains exactly zero across both
available experiments, at every individual grid point, not just in
aggregate.} Across all 14 Experiment~1 grid points and all 6 Experiment~2
settings, \texttt{kabc}'s error rate is $0.0\%$ and its ARI is $1.000$ ---
checked per-point/per-setting, not just as an aggregate that could hide a
few bad cells. This holds at both pilot scale (Experiment~1, 10 reps) and
production scale (Experiment~2, 50 reps), which is reassuring: the earlier,
smaller pilot's perfect-KABC finding was not a small-sample artifact that
washed out at 50 reps.

\paragraph{6.2 KABC-SG's fragility (documented in the previous report) is
confirmed again under the redesigned grids, and is now clearly
family-/point-dependent rather than uniform.} In Experiment~1,
\texttt{kabc-sg} fails to converge at all at the hardest points ($\Delta=1$,
$d\in\{50,100\}$: $0/10$ reps stopped, $\tau$ pinned at the doubling
trick's $277{,}440$ ceiling) while converging easily elsewhere. In
Experiment~2, the pattern is different but equally sharp: \texttt{kabc-sg}
converges \emph{often} on \texttt{moment\_matched\_mixture} ($92$--$98\%$)
but its ARI there is only $0.18$--$0.23$ --- a confident-wrong-answer
failure mode, not a budget-exhaustion one, distinct from Experiment~1's
failure mode on the same algorithm. Both are consistent with the mechanism
established during calibration (\texttt{docs/experiments.md} \S0.2):
\texttt{kabc-sg}'s single, non-adaptive threshold cannot tighten itself
against a specific instance's own variance/shape structure the way
\texttt{kabc}'s per-pair empirical threshold does.

\paragraph{6.3 ACB is not merely imprecise on same-mean data --- it is
exactly, provably blind, and this shows up identically at every one of
Experiment~2's 6 settings.} \texttt{acb}/\texttt{acb-0.9x}'s ARI is exactly
$0.000$ and stopped rate exactly $0\%$ at every single setting individually
(not just in the 6-setting aggregate), with $\tau$ landing exactly on the
theoretical \texttt{budget\_cap} bound each time (Figure~\ref{fig:exp2-fig1}).
This is the sharpest possible confirmation of the paper's motivating claim
on synthetic data: a mean-based method has zero signal when clusters share
a mean, regardless of how much budget it is given or how the shape/geometry
of the mismatch varies.

\paragraph{6.4 The \texttt{-0.9x} self-comparison rows show real,
non-trivial margin for every algorithm tested, in both experiments ---
none of the three stopping rules examined here are ``mostly slack.''}
\texttt{kabc}'s own ARI at 90\% of its realized budget drops to $0.027$
(Experiment~1) and $0.123$ (Experiment~2) --- far from the $1.000$ its full
budget achieves. \texttt{kabc-sg} and \texttt{acb} show the same direction
of effect. This is a genuinely new finding relative to the previous
report's restricted-budget pass (which used a fixed $0.25\times$ multiplier
applied uniformly, rather than each algorithm's own realized $\tau$): even
a modest, self-relative 10\% cut meaningfully hurts every adaptive method
tested so far, suggesting these stopping rules are calibrated close to the
minimum budget actually needed, not with large unused margin.

\paragraph{6.5 Active/adaptive selection's edge over naive uniform sampling
at a matched budget is real but modest, on the settings tested so far.}
\texttt{unif-kkm-1x} (tied to \texttt{kabc}'s own $\tau$) reaches ARI
$1.000$ in Experiment~1 and $0.987$ in Experiment~2, both very close to
\texttt{kabc}'s own $1.000$. This says that, once \texttt{kabc} has decided
how much budget a problem needs, spending that same budget \emph{without}
kabc's adaptive arm-selection (just uniform pulls + kernel $k$-means) is
already most of the way to as good --- the advantage of adaptivity here
shows up more in \emph{how much budget is needed at all} (the $\tau$
figures) than in accuracy at a budget already known to be sufficient.

\paragraph{6.6 Open items for the rework.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Experiment~3's results are entirely pending (Section~\ref{sec:results-exp3})
    --- a 50-rep run under the current \texttt{-0.9x} naming and REVISION 4
    thresholds is the single largest gap in this report.
  \item Experiment~1 is still at pilot scale (10 reps); the canonical
    20-rep first pass (\texttt{configs/exp1.yaml}) has not yet been run to
    completion and would give tighter error bars, especially at the hard
    points ($\Delta=1$, $d\in\{50,100\}$) where this pilot's std.\ devs.\
    are already visibly large on only 10 reps.
  \item \texttt{kabc-sg}'s and \texttt{acb}'s calibrated constants
    (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) were validated against a single fixed
    calibration instance and are not re-validated against either
    experiment's actual hard points here --- the error/ARI numbers above
    should be read as ``as calibrated in this repo,'' not as a claim about
    the underlying algorithms' theoretical guarantees.
  \item This report does not yet include a per-setting/per-grid-point
    breakdown table (only the aggregate table and the figures) --- worth
    adding once Experiment~3's data lands, so all three experiments get the
    same level of detail.
\end{itemize}
